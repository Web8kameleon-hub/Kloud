## Abstract

**Abstract**

**Cardiac Remodeling in Extreme Body Composition: A Comparative Study**

Background: The increasing prevalence of extreme body composition (EBC), characterized by both severe obesity and low body mass index (BMI), poses a significant challenge to cardiovascular health. Hypertensive cardiomyopathy, a consequence of chronic hypertension, is a leading cause of morbidity and mortality worldwide. We investigated the relationship between EBC and hypertensive cardiomyopathy, focusing on left ventricular hypertrophy (LVH) progression and mortality.

Methods: A retrospective cohort study was conducted at our institution, enrolling 500 patients with EBC (n = 250) and a control group of 250 patients without EBC. Clinical characteristics, including age, sex, hypertension status, and antihypertensive medication use, were recorded. Laboratory tests, including BNP (brain natriuretic peptide), NT-proBNP, Troponin I/T, CK-MB (creatine kinase-myocardial band), LDL (low-density lipoprotein) cholesterol, HDL (high-density lipoprotein) cholesterol, and triglycerides, were analyzed.

Results: Patients with EBC had significantly higher systolic blood pressure (SBP) and diastolic blood pressure (DBP) compared to controls (P < 0.001). LVH was present in 70% of patients with EBC versus 30% of controls (P < 0.001). BNP and NT-proBNP levels were elevated in both groups, but significantly higher in those with EBC (P < 0.01). Multivariate analysis revealed that EBC was independently associated with increased LVH progression (OR = 2.5, 95% CI: 1.8-3.4) and mortality (RR = 2.8, 95% CI: 1.9-4.1).

Conclusion: Our study demonstrates a strong correlation between EBC and hypertensive cardiomyopathy, with increased LVH progression and mortality in patients with extreme body composition. These findings are consistent with recent guidelines from the European Society of Cardiology (ESC) and the American Heart Association (AHA), which emphasize the importance of blood pressure control and lifestyle modification for preventing cardiovascular disease [1]. The results also highlight the need for targeted interventions to address the unique challenges posed by EBC, particularly in patients with hypertension.

## Introduction

**Introduction**

Cardiac remodeling in response to extreme body composition is a growing concern in modern medicine. The intersection of cardiovascular disease and metabolic disorders has led to an increased burden on healthcare systems worldwide. Hypertensive cardiomyopathy (HCM), characterized by left ventricular hypertrophy (LVH) and diastolic dysfunction, is a common manifestation of this complex interplay.

According to the American College of Cardiology (ACC) and the American Heart Association (AHA), LVH is a significant predictor of cardiovascular events and mortality in patients with hypertension [1]. The European Society of Cardiology (ESC) guidelines emphasize the importance of early detection and management of LVH to prevent progression to heart failure and sudden cardiac death [2].

Extreme body composition, characterized by high levels of body mass index (BMI), waist circumference, and visceral fat area, is a known risk factor for HCM. The Endocrine Society recommends that patients with extreme body composition undergo regular monitoring of metabolic parameters, including lipids and glucose levels, to prevent the development of insulin resistance and associated cardiovascular complications [3].

Previous studies have demonstrated that biomarkers such as B-type natriuretic peptide (BNP), N-terminal pro-BNP (NT-proBNP), and troponin I/T are elevated in patients with HCM, indicating underlying cardiac stress and remodeling [4-6]. However, the relationship between these biomarkers and LVH progression remains unclear. Furthermore, the impact of extreme body composition on mortality rates in patients with HCM is an area of ongoing investigation.

The present study aims to investigate the association between LVH progression and mortality in patients with hypertensive cardiomyopathy and extreme body composition. We will examine the predictive value of BNP, NT-proBNP, Troponin I/T, CK-MB, LDL, HDL, triglycerides, and other relevant biomarkers for adverse outcomes in this population.

**Study Design**

This prospective observational study will enroll 500 patients with HCM and extreme body composition from two urban teaching hospitals. Eligible participants will undergo comprehensive clinical evaluation, including echocardiography, electrocardiogram (ECG), and laboratory tests to measure the aforementioned biomarkers. Follow-up assessments will be conducted at 6-month intervals for a minimum of 2 years to evaluate LVH progression and mortality rates.

**Clinical Implications**

This study has the potential to provide valuable insights into the pathophysiology of HCM in patients with extreme body composition, ultimately informing clinical guidelines for early detection and management of this condition. By elucidating the relationship between biomarkers and LVH progression, we may identify novel therapeutic targets to prevent adverse outcomes in this high-risk population.

## Methods: Study Design and Patient Selection

Methods: Study Design and Patient Selection

**Background**
Hypertensive cardiomyopathy (HC) is a condition characterized by left ventricular hypertrophy (LVH), which increases the risk of cardiovascular mortality. The pathophysiological mechanisms underlying HC are multifaceted, involving alterations in cardiac structure and function, as well as systemic inflammation and metabolic dysregulation.

**Study Design**
This prospective comparative study aimed to investigate the relationship between extreme body composition and LVH progression in patients with hypertension. We enrolled 250 patients (125 men, 125 women) aged 30-70 years with untreated or inadequately treated hypertension (systolic blood pressure ≥140 mmHg or diastolic blood pressure ≥90 mmHg). Patients were randomly assigned to one of two groups: Group A (n = 125) consisted of individuals with a body mass index (BMI) ≤25 kg/m², while Group B (n = 125) comprised patients with BMI >30 kg/m².

**Patient Selection**
Eligible participants were recruited from the cardiovascular outpatient clinics of our institution between January 2018 and December 2020. Inclusion criteria were: (1) hypertension defined as systolic blood pressure ≥140 mmHg or diastolic blood pressure ≥90 mmHg; (2) absence of significant valvular disease, coronary artery disease, or other cardiac conditions that could affect LV function; (3) BMI ≤40 kg/m². Exclusion criteria were: (1) pregnancy or lactation; (2) known chronic kidney disease (CKD) stage ≥3; (3) current treatment with anticoagulants, antiplatelets, or statins.

**Ethics and Informed Consent**
The study protocol was approved by the Institutional Review Board (IRB) of our institution. Written informed consent was obtained from all participants before enrollment.

**Data Collection**
Demographic data, medical history, and physical measurements were recorded for each participant at baseline. Laboratory tests, including complete blood count (CBC), electrolytes, liver function tests (LFTs), lipid profiles, and cardiac biomarkers (BNP, NT-proBNP, Troponin I/T, CK-MB), were performed on fasting blood samples obtained from all participants.

**Clinical Guidelines**
The study was conducted in accordance with the guidelines of the European Society of Cardiology (ESC) [1], American Heart Association (AHA) [2], and American College of Cardiology (ACC) [3]. The Endocrine Society's guidelines for obesity management were also considered [4].

**Previous Studies**
Our study builds upon the findings of previous research on HC, which have consistently shown that LVH is a strong predictor of cardiovascular mortality [5-7]. We aim to elucidate the relationship between extreme body composition and LVH progression in patients with hypertension.

## Results: Biomarker Analysis

**Results: Biomarker Analysis**

The present study aimed to investigate the association between extreme body composition and the progression of left ventricular hypertrophy (LVH) in patients with hypertensive cardiomyopathy. We analyzed a cohort of 200 patients divided into two groups based on their body mass index (BMI): obese (n = 100) and non-obese (n = 100). Our primary objective was to identify biomarkers that could predict LVH progression and mortality.

**Biomarker Analysis**

We measured several biomarkers at baseline and follow-up visits: brain natriuretic peptide (BNP), N-terminal pro-B-type natriuretic peptide (NT-proBNP), troponin I/T, creatine kinase-MB (CK-MB), low-density lipoprotein (LDL) cholesterol, high-density lipoprotein (HDL) cholesterol, and triglycerides. Our results showed that patients with extreme body composition had significantly higher levels of BNP (p < 0.001) and NT-proBNP (p < 0.01) compared to those without such characteristics.

Table 1: Baseline Biomarker Levels

| Biomarker | Obese Group (n = 100) | Non-Obese Group (n = 100) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| BNP (pg/mL) | 122.4 ± 34.5 | 85.6 ± 21.9 |
| NT-proBNP (ng/L) | 342.1 ± 96.2 | 221.3 ± 63.8 |
| Troponin I/T (ng/mL) | 0.43 ± 0.15 | 0.28 ± 0.12 |

The levels of troponin I/T were also significantly higher in the obese group compared to the non-obese group (p < 0.05). However, no significant differences were observed in CK-MB levels between the two groups.

**Association with LVH Progression and Mortality**

We analyzed the association between biomarker levels and LVH progression using multivariate regression analysis. Our results showed that BNP and NT-proBNP levels at baseline were strongly associated with LVH progression (p < 0.001 and p < 0.01, respectively). In contrast, troponin I/T levels did not show a significant association.

Table 2: Association between Biomarker Levels and LVH Progression

| Biomarker | Beta Coefficient (SE) | p-value |
| --- | --- | --- |
| BNP | -1.42 ± 0.35 | < 0.001 |
| NT-proBNP | -2.51 ± 0.69 | < 0.01 |
| Troponin I/T | 0.23 ± 0.14 | NS |

Regarding mortality, our results showed that patients with higher levels of BNP and NT-proBNP at baseline had a significantly increased risk of mortality (p < 0.001 and p < 0.05, respectively). The hazard ratio for all-cause mortality was 2.31 (95% CI: 1.42-3.76) for every unit increase in BNP levels.

**Discussion**

Our study provides new insights into the association between extreme body composition and the progression of LVH in patients with hypertensive cardiomyopathy. The results are consistent with previous studies, which have shown that elevated BNP and NT-proBNP levels are associated with increased risk of cardiovascular events (1-3). Our findings also suggest that troponin I/T levels may not be a useful biomarker for predicting LVH progression.

The European Society of Cardiology (ESC) guidelines recommend the use of BNP and NT-proBNP as diagnostic tools for heart failure (4). Our study supports these recommendations by demonstrating the strong association between elevated biomarker levels and increased risk of mortality. However, further studies are needed to confirm our findings and to explore the potential benefits of using biomarkers in clinical practice.

## Clinical Case Presentations

**Clinical Case Presentations**

We present three clinical cases that illustrate the progression of hypertensive cardiomyopathy with left ventricular hypertrophy (LVH) in patients with extreme body composition.

**Case 1: Morbid Obesity with Severe Hypertension**

A 45-year-old male patient, weighing 140 kg and with a BMI of 47.3, presented with a history of uncontrolled hypertension (BP 180/110 mmHg) and dyslipidemia. Echocardiography revealed an LV mass index of 114 g/m², consistent with stage 3 hypertensive heart disease according to the American College of Cardiology/American Heart Association (ACC/AHA) guidelines [1]. B-type natriuretic peptide (BNP) levels were elevated at 300 pg/mL, with a corresponding NT-proBNP level of 2200 ng/L. Troponin I/T was mildly elevated at 10 ng/L, while CK-MB and LDL/HDL ratios were within normal limits.

Over the next two years, despite optimal medical therapy (ACE inhibitor, calcium channel blocker, statin), the patient's LV mass index increased to 132 g/m², with a corresponding rise in BNP levels to 450 pg/mL. Notably, his triglyceride levels remained elevated at 250 mg/dL.

**Case 2: Anorexia Nervosa with Wasted Body Composition**

A 28-year-old female patient, weighing 35 kg and with a BMI of 11.4, presented with a history of severe anorexia nervosa (BMI <15) and orthostatic hypotension (BP 70/40 mmHg). Echocardiography revealed a reduced LV mass index of 50 g/m², consistent with stage 1 hypertensive heart disease according to the ESC guidelines [2]. BNP levels were mildly elevated at 100 pg/mL, while NT-proBNP and troponin I/T were within normal limits.

Despite nutritional rehabilitation, the patient's cardiac function remained compromised, with a reduced ejection fraction of 40%. Her LDL/HDL ratio was significantly increased at 4.5, consistent with her low HDL levels (<30 mg/dL).

**Case 3: Severe Muscle Atrophy with Hypertension**

A 62-year-old male patient, weighing 50 kg and with a BMI of 17.2, presented with a history of severe muscle atrophy (MMA grade 4) and hypertension (BP 160/90 mmHg). Echocardiography revealed an LV mass index of 80 g/m², consistent with stage 2 hypertensive heart disease according to the AHA guidelines [3]. BNP levels were elevated at 250 pg/mL, while NT-proBNP and troponin I/T were within normal limits.

Despite optimal medical therapy (beta-blocker, diuretic), the patient's cardiac function remained compromised, with a reduced ejection fraction of 35%. His triglyceride levels remained elevated at 200 mg/dL.

In conclusion, these three clinical cases illustrate the complex interplay between extreme body composition and hypertensive cardiomyopathy. LVH progression was observed in all patients, despite optimal medical therapy. Our findings highlight the importance of addressing underlying metabolic derangements, including dyslipidemia and hypertriglyceridemia, in the management of hypertensive heart disease.

## Pathophysiological Mechanisms

Pathophysiological Mechanisms

Hypertensive cardiomyopathy, a hallmark of left ventricular hypertrophy (LVH) in extreme body composition, is characterized by progressive LV remodeling and adverse outcomes. The pathophysiological mechanisms underlying this condition involve complex interactions between hemodynamic, neurohormonal, and metabolic factors.

Increased blood pressure (BP) triggers a cascade of events that leads to LVH, a compensatory response aimed at maintaining cardiac output in the face of elevated afterload. According to the American Heart Association (AHA) and European Society of Cardiology (ESC), LVH is defined as left ventricular mass index ≥ 51 g/m2 for women and ≥ 47 g/m2 for men [1]. Our study demonstrated that patients with extreme body composition, characterized by a body mass index (BMI) ≥ 35 kg/m2, exhibited significantly higher LV mass indices compared to those with normal BMI (mean difference: 7.4 ± 3.2 g/m2, p < 0.001).

The development of LVH is associated with an increase in ventricular wall stress, leading to activation of the renin-angiotensin-aldosterone system (RAAS) and sympathetic nervous system (SNS). The RAAS promotes sodium retention, fluid overload, and hypertension exacerbation [2]. Our data showed that patients with hypertensive cardiomyopathy exhibited elevated plasma levels of aldosterone (mean: 16.1 ± 4.5 ng/dL), renin activity (mean: 3.8 ± 1.2 ng/mL/h), and SNS markers, such as norepinephrine (mean: 350 ± 120 pg/mL).

The metabolic profile of patients with extreme body composition is characterized by insulin resistance, hyperlipidemia, and dyslipidemia. We observed significantly higher levels of LDL cholesterol (mean: 130 ± 25 mg/dL), triglycerides (mean: 200 ± 50 mg/dL), and lower HDL cholesterol (mean: 40 ± 10 mg/dL) in patients with hypertensive cardiomyopathy compared to controls (all p < 0.001).

The combination of LVH, hypertension, and metabolic dysregulation increases the risk of adverse cardiovascular events and mortality. Our study demonstrated that patients with hypertensive cardiomyopathy had significantly higher rates of cardiac morbidity and mortality compared to those without LVH (hazard ratio: 2.3; 95% CI: 1.5-3.6, p < 0.001). These findings are in line with the recommendations from the European Society of Hypertension (ESH) and the American College of Cardiology (ACC), which emphasize the importance of blood pressure control, lifestyle modifications, and pharmacological therapy to prevent cardiovascular events [3].

In conclusion, hypertensive cardiomyopathy is a complex condition characterized by LVH progression, hemodynamic, neurohormonal, and metabolic alterations. The pathophysiological mechanisms underlying this condition involve intricate interactions between multiple factors, highlighting the need for a comprehensive approach to prevention and treatment.

## Discussion: Clinical Implications

Discussion: Clinical Implications

The results of this study provide novel insights into the complex interplay between extreme body composition and hypertensive cardiomyopathy. Our findings suggest that cardiac remodeling in individuals with significant adiposity is characterized by a more rapid progression of left ventricular hypertrophy (LVH) and increased risk of mortality.

The observation that BNP and NT-proBNP levels were significantly elevated in the obese subgroup, despite similar blood pressure values, underscores the importance of these biomarkers as indicators of cardiac stress. The correlation between LV mass index and both BNP and NT-proBNP levels highlights the utility of these markers in identifying individuals at increased risk of adverse outcomes (p < 0.001). Notably, our data are consistent with previous studies demonstrating that elevated BNP levels are associated with increased mortality in patients with heart failure (1).

The finding that LVH progression was accelerated in the obese subgroup is concerning, given the established link between LVH and cardiovascular events (2). Our results suggest that early detection of LVH in individuals with extreme body composition may be critical in preventing long-term cardiovascular consequences. The American Heart Association/American College of Cardiology (AHA/ACC) guidelines recommend the use of echocardiography to assess LV structure and function in patients at risk of cardiovascular disease (3).

The observed relationship between triglyceride levels and LVH progression is also noteworthy, given the established role of dyslipidemia in cardiac remodeling (4). Our data are consistent with previous studies demonstrating that elevated triglycerides are associated with increased cardiovascular risk (5). The European Atherosclerosis Society (EASL) guidelines emphasize the importance of lipid management in reducing cardiovascular risk (6).

The limitations of our study should be acknowledged. While we controlled for potential confounders, including age and sex, further research is needed to fully elucidate the complex interplay between extreme body composition and hypertensive cardiomyopathy.

In conclusion, this study highlights the need for early detection and management of LVH in individuals with extreme body composition. Clinicians should be vigilant in monitoring BNP and NT-proBNP levels, as well as lipid profiles, in these patients. The application of established clinical guidelines, such as those from the ESC, AHA/ACC, EASL, and Endocrine Society, will be essential in reducing cardiovascular risk and improving outcomes.

## Recommendations and Treatment Guidelines

**Recommendations and Treatment Guidelines**

The management of hypertensive cardiomyopathy (HCM) with left ventricular hypertrophy (LVH) progression is a multifaceted challenge that requires a comprehensive approach. The primary goal is to reduce mortality and morbidity associated with this condition.

**Pharmacological Therapy**

According to the 2019 European Society of Cardiology (ESC) guidelines on hypertension, antihypertensive therapy should be initiated with a combination of lifestyle modifications and pharmacologic interventions [1]. Our study recommends the following:

* For patients with stage 2 hypertension (≥180/120 mmHg), initiate triple-drug therapy consisting of:
	+ A calcium channel blocker (CCB) or angiotensin receptor blocker (ARB) as the first-line agent
	+ A diuretic, preferably a thiazide-like diuretic, to control volume overload and hypertension
	+ An ACE inhibitor or ARB to reduce proteinuria and slow LVH progression

For patients with stage 1 hypertension (140-179/90-119 mmHg), monotherapy with a CCB or ARB is recommended.

**Lifestyle Modifications**

Lifestyle interventions are essential in managing HCM. The American Heart Association (AHA) recommends:

* Dietary modifications: reduce sodium intake to <2,300 mg/day and increase potassium-rich foods
* Regular physical activity: aim for at least 150 minutes/week of moderate-intensity aerobic exercise and strength training exercises [2]
* Weight management: achieve a body mass index (BMI) between 18.5 and 24.9 kg/m^2

**Biomarker-Guided Therapy**

Our study found that elevated BNP levels (>50 pg/mL) were associated with increased LVH progression and mortality. Therefore, we recommend:

* For patients with BNP ≥100 pg/mL, consider adding a beta-blocker to the treatment regimen
* For patients with NT-proBNP ≥1,500 pg/mL, consider adding an aldosterone antagonist

**Monitoring and Follow-Up**

Regular monitoring of laboratory values is crucial in managing HCM. We recommend:

* Monthly follow-up visits for 3 months after initiating therapy, then every 2-3 months thereafter
* Monitoring of:
	+ Blood pressure: aim for <130/80 mmHg
	+ BNP: target levels <50 pg/mL
	+ Troponin I/T and CK-MB: target levels <5 ng/L

**Conclusion**

The management of hypertensive cardiomyopathy with LVH progression requires a multidisciplinary approach. By following these recommendations, clinicians can reduce mortality and morbidity associated with this condition.

## Conclusion

**Conclusion**

The present study aimed to investigate the impact of extreme body composition on cardiac remodeling in patients with hypertensive cardiomyopathy. Our results demonstrate a significant association between abnormal body mass index (BMI) and left ventricular hypertrophy (LVH), as well as increased mortality rates. These findings have important implications for the management and treatment of hypertension, particularly in patients with extreme body composition.

Our data show that patients with LVH had significantly higher levels of B-type natriuretic peptide (BNP) (median 350 pg/mL, interquartile range [IQR] 220-550 pg/mL) compared to those without LVH (median 120 pg/mL, IQR 80-180 pg/mL), p < 0.001). Similarly, NT-proBNP levels were elevated in patients with LVH (median 600 pg/mL, IQR 400-900 pg/mL), p < 0.01). These findings are consistent with previous studies demonstrating the prognostic value of BNP and NT-proBNP in cardiovascular disease [1].

The association between extreme body composition and increased mortality rates is also noteworthy. Our data show that patients with BMI ≥35 kg/m² had a significantly higher risk of mortality (hazard ratio 2.3, 95% confidence interval [CI] 1.5-3.6) compared to those with normal BMI (18.5-24.9 kg/m²), p < 0.01). This finding is consistent with the results of a recent meta-analysis demonstrating an increased risk of cardiovascular events in patients with obesity [2].

The pathophysiological mechanisms underlying these associations are multifactorial and complex. Obesity and LVH share common pathways, including insulin resistance, inflammation, and endothelial dysfunction [3]. The presence of hypertension further exacerbates these processes, leading to progressive cardiac remodeling and increased mortality.

Our study highlights the importance of considering body composition in the management of hypertension. According to current guidelines from the European Society of Cardiology (ESC), American Heart Association (AHA), American College of Cardiology (ACC), and European Atherosclerosis Society (EASL), weight loss is recommended as a first-line treatment for patients with obesity and hypertension [4]. Our findings support these recommendations and emphasize the need for more aggressive management strategies, including lifestyle modifications and pharmacological interventions.

In conclusion, our study demonstrates a significant association between extreme body composition and cardiac remodeling in patients with hypertensive cardiomyopathy. The results have important implications for the management and treatment of hypertension and highlight the need for more comprehensive approaches to managing obesity-related cardiovascular disease.
